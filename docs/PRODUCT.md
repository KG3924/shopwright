# Product

Shopwright exists to give a builder *confidence to cut*.

Most people will never own a clean set of dimensioned plans. They will photograph a bench at a friend’s house, screenshot a console on a product page, or hold up a phone in a showroom. The job of this app is to turn that image into a shop packet they can argue with.

## Principles

1. **Interpretation, not cloning.** The output is a piece a competent shop would build in the same spirit and size. It is not a reproduction of factory internals.
2. **The graph is the product.** Tutorials are compiled from parts, joints, hardware, and a route — not written as a one-off essay per photo.
3. **Uncertainty is a first-class object.** If the underside is invisible, the builder picks a construction route. Confidence is visible. Warnings are visible.
4. **Rank changes the packet, not the piece.** A beginner Shaker bench is still that bench. Pocket holes replace mortises; overall size stays.
5. **Size is parametric.** An alcove that is 62" wide is a slider, not a new design. Dependent parts move.
6. **Wood has consequences.** Species is not a stain swatch. It is weight, movement, weather, fasteners, and a lumber stop.

## The builder

The bulk user is not a CAD operator. They have a circular saw, a drill, maybe a Kreg jig. They live near a Home Depot and, if they’re lucky, a hardwood dealer. They need a buy list they can take to Allen, Texas on a Saturday morning.

The secondary user is a craftsman who wants the same piece compiled for mortise-and-tenon and a walnut bill of materials.

## Voice

Quiet. Specific. No emoji. No “you’ve got this!”. A master at the bench telling you where the joint will fail.
